# BRC-256: Cross-Chain Atomic Swaps Between Wallets

Brandon Cryderman / HandCash (brandongcryderman@gmail.com)

**Status:** Draft (proposed number, not a BSVA assignment)

## Abstract

This document lets two [BRC-100](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/BRCs/blob/master/wallet/0100.md) wallets swap coins of two different chains, BSV and the chains of draft BRC-213, directly with each other. Nobody holds either side's coins at any point. Either both get paid, or each takes its own coins back after a lock time.

1. **Offer.** A maker signs a standing price: it gives one asset and wants another, for any amount between two bounds, until an expiry. Anyone may host or relay the offer. Every wallet checks the signature, the bounds and the expiry itself.
2. **Take.** A taker proposes terms for one amount: the swap id, the hash of a secret only it knows, its per-swap key and two lock times. The maker's wallet checks the terms against its own offer and accepts once.
3. **Lock.** The taker locks what it pays, on the chain the maker wants, to a hash-locked output (HTLC) that stands for 36 hours. Once that lock is buried, the maker locks what it gives, on the other chain, for 12 hours.
4. **Claim.** The taker claims the maker's lock with the secret, which puts the secret on that chain. The maker reads it there and claims the taker's lock.
5. **Refund.** A lock that is not claimed in time goes back to its payer. On the BRC-213 chains `OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY` enforces this. BSV has no time-lock opcode, so a BSV lock's refund needs a co-signature, which the counterparty gives before the lock is broadcast.

Messages between the two wallets are [BRC-78](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/BRCs/blob/master/peer-to-peer/0078.md) sealed and ride a [BRC-33](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/BRCs/blob/master/peer-to-peer/0033.md) message box. A service fee, when a wallet charges one, is a third output on one leg, opened by the same secret. It can be claimed only if the swap happened, and otherwise returns to its payer.

## Motivation

Moving value between chains today means trusting a party in the middle: an exchange, a bridge, or a wrapped token's issuer. Each one holds the user's coins for a while, and each one can fail, freeze or disappear with them.

Two UTXO chains need no middle party. A hash lock on each side, opened by one secret, makes the swap atomic. A time lock on each side makes it safe to walk away. What is missing is the agreement on details that two different wallets need in order to swap with each other:

- the exact scripts on every chain, including BSV, which has no time-lock opcode;
- who locks first, for how long, and when each side must act;
- the messages, their encryption, and where they travel;
- how an offer is signed, so that a relay or index cannot alter it;
- how a fee can be charged without anyone holding the coins.

This document fixes those details. Its guarantees:

1. Neither wallet ever signs anything that lets the other take its coins without paying.
2. Every path that locks coins ends in a claim or a refund. Every refusal before a lock ends with nothing moved.
3. A wallet decides depth, amounts and secrets from bytes it checks itself, never from an explorer's answer.
4. A host can delay or drop messages and offers, but it cannot alter, forge or steal anything.

## Terms

- **Asset:** `"bsv"` or a BRC-213 chain id (`"ltc"`, `"bch"`, `"btc"`, `"doge"`). Amounts are decimal strings of the asset's base units.
- **Maker:** the wallet that signed the offer. It gives `give` and wants `get`.
- **Taker:** the wallet that takes the offer. It pays `get` and receives `give`.
- **Leg:** one side's lock. The **taker leg** is on `get`, paid by the taker. The **maker leg** is on `give`, paid by the maker.
- **Secret:** 32 bytes only the taker knows until it claims. **Hash:** its SHA-256.
- **T1, T2:** the lock times of the taker leg and the maker leg, as Unix seconds. T1 is later than T2.
- **Buried:** a leg's lock is in a block that the wallet has checked: at depth `D` on its own header chain for a BRC-213 chain (BRC-213 §4), or with a merkle path that verifies under BRC-67 for BSV.

## Specification

### 1. Offers

An offer is a JSON object:

```json
{
  "v": 1,
  "maker": "<maker identity key, compressed hex>",
  "give": "ltc",
  "get": "bsv",
  "price": { "give": "28000000", "get": "100000000" },
  "minGet": "3500000",
  "maxGet": "3500000",
  "createdAt": 1800000000000,
  "expiresAt": 1800003600000,
  "nonce": "<32 random bytes, hex>",
  "messagebox": "https://box.example/v1/messagebox",
  "sig": "<DER signature, hex>"
}
```

- `give` and `get` are different assets.
- A taker pays any amount `pay` of `get` with `minGet ≤ pay ≤ maxGet` and receives `floor(pay × price.give / price.get)` of `give`.
- `createdAt` and `expiresAt` are Unix milliseconds. An offer stands for at most 24 hours.
- `messagebox` is optional: it names the BRC-33 host where takers reach the maker. Without it, a taker uses the host it would use for the maker's identity.

**Signature.** The maker signs SHA-256 of the UTF-8 string `"handcash swap offer v1\n"` followed by the JSON array `[v, maker, give, get, price.give, price.get, minGet, maxGet, createdAt, expiresAt, nonce, messagebox or ""]`, serialized with no whitespace. The signing key is the BRC-42 child of the maker's identity key under protocol `[2, "swap order"]`, key ID `nonce` and counterparty `anyone`.

The protocol is reserved for the wallet. A wallet MUST refuse any application request to derive, sign or reveal a key under it, so that an application holding a signing grant cannot publish offers in the user's name.

**Checking.** A wallet MUST check an offer before it shows it or takes it, and MUST refuse the offer when any of these fails:

1. Every field has its shape: compressed keys, 64-hex nonce, positive integer amounts, `minGet ≤ maxGet`, and an `https` URL for `messagebox`.
2. The current time is before `expiresAt`.
3. `createdAt` is at most 5 minutes after the current time.
4. `expiresAt − createdAt` is at most 24 hours.
5. The signature verifies against the maker's derived public key.

**Cancel.** A maker withdraws an offer by signing SHA-256 of `"handcash swap offer cancel v1\n" + nonce` with the same derived key. A host that lists offers MUST accept a cancel only with that signature.

**Hosting (informative).** Any host can list offers: it verifies each one on publish, may cap how many open offers and publishes a maker can have, and removes offers that expire or are cancelled. A host is never trusted. A wallet checks every listed offer again itself.

### 2. Lock scripts

A lock pays whoever shows the secret and signs with `claimPub`. After `locktime` it returns to its payer. Every key is a compressed secp256k1 key. `locktime` is Unix seconds, at least 500000000 and at most 2^32 − 1, so that `nLockTime` reads it as a time.

**BRC-213 chains:**

```
OP_IF
  OP_SIZE <32> OP_EQUALVERIFY OP_SHA256 <hash> OP_EQUALVERIFY <claimPub>
OP_ELSE
  <locktime> OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY OP_DROP <refundPub>
OP_ENDIF
OP_CHECKSIG
```

**BSV:**

```
OP_IF
  OP_SIZE <32> OP_EQUALVERIFY OP_SHA256 <hash> OP_EQUALVERIFY <claimPub> OP_CHECKSIG
OP_ELSE
  <refundPub> OP_CHECKSIGVERIFY <cosignPub> OP_CHECKSIG
OP_ENDIF
```

`OP_SIZE <32> OP_EQUALVERIFY` makes the secret exactly 32 bytes on every chain, so a secret that opens one leg always opens the other. `<locktime>` is a minimally encoded script number. On BSV, `cosignPub` is the counterparty's per-swap key.

| Asset | Output | Signature hash |
|---|---|---|
| `ltc`, `btc` | P2WSH of the script | BIP143, `SIGHASH_ALL` |
| `bch` | P2SH of the script | BIP143, `SIGHASH_ALL\|FORKID` |
| `doge` | P2SH of the script | Original signature hash, `SIGHASH_ALL` |
| `bsv` | The script itself as the locking script | BIP143 with `FORKID` (`0x41`) |

**Spending.** A claim or refund spends one or more lock outputs of one transaction to one output of the spender's own.

- A claim unlocks with `<sig> <secret> OP_1` and has `nLockTime` 0.
- A refund unlocks with `<sig> OP_0` on BRC-213 chains and `<cosig> <sig> OP_0` on BSV, and has `nLockTime` equal to `locktime`.
- On P2WSH the items are witness items followed by the script. On P2SH the script is pushed last in the scriptSig.
- Every input has sequence `0xfffffffe`. Transactions are version 2 on BRC-213 chains and version 1 on BSV.

**Reading the secret.** A wallet finds the secret in a claim by taking each 32-byte data item of the spending input (witness items, or scriptSig pushes) and keeping the one whose SHA-256 is the hash. It MUST NOT trust a secret it has not hashed.

### 3. Keys and the secret

- **Per-swap key.** Each wallet claims, refunds and co-signs with the BRC-42 child of its identity key under protocol `[2, "swap settle"]`, key ID the swap id and counterparty `self`.
- **Secret.** The taker's secret is SHA-256 of the 32-byte private key of the BRC-42 child under protocol `[2, "swap secret"]`, key ID the swap id and counterparty `self`. A wallet restored from its root mid-swap therefore still has both.

Both protocols are reserved for the wallet like `[2, "swap order"]` (§1).

### 4. Terms and the take

The taker proposes terms:

```json
{
  "v": 1,
  "swapId": "<16 random bytes, hex>",
  "offer": { "...": "the signed offer, unchanged" },
  "taker": "<taker identity key>",
  "pay": "3500000",
  "receive": "980000",
  "fee": { "leg": "taker", "amount": "17500" },
  "hash": "<SHA-256 of the secret, hex>",
  "takerPub": "<taker per-swap key>",
  "takenAt": 1800000100000,
  "t1": 1800129700,
  "t2": 1800043300
}
```

- `receive` is `floor(pay × price.give / price.get)`, before any fee on the maker leg.
- `fee` is the service fee of §8, or `null`.
- `t1 = floor(takenAt / 1000) + 129600` (36 hours) and `t2 = floor(takenAt / 1000) + 43200` (12 hours).

**The maker's check.** Before it locks anything, the maker's wallet MUST refuse a take, answering `decline` with the reason, when any of these fails:

1. The terms have their shape (`malformed`).
2. The offer checks under §1 and is the maker's own (`offer`). The taker is not the maker (`own-offer`).
3. `pay` is within the offer's bounds (`out-of-bounds`), and `receive` is exactly what the price gives (`wrong-receive`).
4. `fee` is exactly what §8 gives, or both are `null` (`wrong-fee`).
5. `takenAt` is within 5 minutes of the maker's clock (`stale`), and `t1` and `t2` are exactly as above (`wrong-times`).
6. What the taker receives exceeds the cost of one claim on `give` (`nothing-left`).
7. The sender of the message is `taker` (`sender`).
8. The offer is still open in the maker's own records (`offer-<state>`, such as `offer-taken`, or `offer-unknown`), and the maker can fund its leg (`insufficient`). A take repeating a swap id the maker already holds is ignored.

A wallet SHOULD answer takes of its own open offers without asking the user: publishing the offer was the consent. It accepts a given offer exactly once and then withdraws it from any host. A taker's wallet MUST ask the user to confirm explicitly before it sends a take. The prompt shows what the taker locks, what it receives, the fee and the lock times.

**Accept.** The maker answers `accept` with its per-swap key `makerPub`. From then on both wallets hold the same **agreed terms**: the terms plus `makerPub`.

### 5. Legs

| Leg | Asset | Payer | Payee | Main output | Lock time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Taker | `offer.get` | Taker | Maker | `pay` | `t1` |
| Maker | `offer.give` | Maker | Taker | `receive`, less the fee if it is on this leg | `t2` |

Each leg's main output is the lock of §2 with `claimPub` the payee's per-swap key, `refundPub` the payer's, and on BSV `cosignPub` the payee's. A leg that carries the fee has a second lock output with the same hash, `refundPub` and lock time, and the fee key as `claimPub` (§8). Both outputs are in the same transaction.

**Order and deadlines.** All times are measured on the wallet's clock against the agreed terms.

| Step | Who | Allowed when |
|---|---|---|
| Fund the taker leg | Taker | After `accept`, within 1 hour of `takenAt` |
| Fund the maker leg | Maker | After the taker leg is buried and checked, and before `t2 − 8 h` |
| Claim the maker leg | Taker | After the maker leg is buried and checked, and before `t2 − 3 h` |
| Claim the taker leg | Maker | Once it knows the secret, until the taker refunds |
| Refund own leg | Payer | At or after its lock time, while it is unclaimed |

A wallet checks a counterparty's lock before acting on it. The lock transaction hashes to its txid, the named outputs pay exactly the agreed scripts and amounts, and the lock is buried. A lock that fails any check is refused, and the wallet does not lock or claim against it. When its own deadline for the next step passes, it waits for its lock time and refunds.

The taker never claims with less than 3 hours left before `t2`, so its claim lands before the maker may refund. The maker never funds later than 8 hours before `t2`, so the taker has time to see the lock buried and claim. A taker that reaches `t2 − 3 h` unpaid waits for `t1` and refunds. The taker leg's longer lock gives the maker at least 24 hours after `t2` to read the secret and claim.

**BSV refund co-signatures.** A payer whose leg is on BSV MUST hold the payee's co-signatures over its refunds before it broadcasts the lock:

1. The payer builds the lock transaction and does not broadcast it. For each lock output it builds the refund: one input spending that output, one output paying a BRC-29 child of its own identity, value fixed at the output's value less 40 satoshis, `nLockTime` the lock time, sequence `0xfffffffe`.
2. It sends `presign` naming the lock txid and each refund's output index, payout script and value.
3. The payee checks that each refund spends an output of the agreed lock, pays at most what the output holds, and carries the leg's lock time. It then signs each refund with its per-swap key and answers `presigned`.
4. The payer verifies every co-signature, records the refunds, and only then broadcasts the lock.

The payee never signs a refund with an earlier `nLockTime`. The payer alone cannot spend the refund path, and the counterparty cannot spend it at all. So a BSV lock is as safe to fund as a time-locked one.

**Journal.** A wallet SHOULD record each lock it funds, and each counterparty lock it may claim, in durable storage before it broadcasts, with enough to claim or refund from its root alone. That is the agreed terms, the outputs and, for BSV, the refunds and their co-signatures. It SHOULD resume every unfinished swap from that record after a restart or restore.

### 6. Messages

Messages are JSON objects, sealed with BRC-78 from the sender's identity to the recipient's, and posted to the recipient's BRC-33 box `swap-settle`. A wallet acknowledges a message only after it has recorded what the message changed.

| `t` | From | Fields | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `take` | Taker | `terms` | Propose terms (§4) |
| `accept` | Maker | `swapId`, `makerPub` | Terms agreed |
| `decline` | Maker | `swapId`, `reason` | Refused before anything was locked |
| `presign` | BSV payer | `swapId`, `leg`, `txid`, `refunds: [{vout, to, value}]` | Co-sign these refunds (§5) |
| `presigned` | BSV payee | `swapId`, `leg`, `cosigs` | The co-signatures, in `refunds` order |
| `funded` | Payer | `swapId`, `leg`, `txid`, `main`, `fee?`, `beef?` | A leg is locked: output indexes of the main and fee outputs, and on BSV the lock's Atomic BEEF |
| `secret` | Taker | `swapId`, `secret` | Courtesy after the taker claims: the maker also reads the secret from the chain |
| `abort` | Either | `swapId`, `reason` | Nothing was locked and nothing will be |

`leg` is `"taker"` or `"maker"`. `to` is a payout script in hex. `cosigs` are DER signatures with their hash type byte, in hex. A message whose `swapId` names no swap the wallet holds, or whose content contradicts the swap's state, is ignored.

Messages are a convenience. Settlement never depends on one arriving. A wallet that misses `funded` can find the lock on the chain, and a maker that misses `secret` reads the secret from the taker's claim. The only exception is the BSV `presign` round trip, which has to finish before the lock is broadcast. Until then nothing is at stake.

### 7. Outcomes

A swap ends in one of five outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `swapped` | Both payees claimed |
| `refunded` | Each payer took its lock back |
| `declined` | The maker refused before anything was locked |
| `expired` | A deadline passed before this wallet locked anything |
| `missed` | The maker was away past `t1` and the taker claimed one leg and refunded the other |

`missed` is the one outcome that costs a party. It costs the maker, who failed to claim within the 24 hours or more it had. A maker wallet SHOULD run a background service while it has an open take, and SHOULD warn the user who closes it with a swap in progress.

### 8. Service fee (optional)

A wallet may charge a fee on swaps it takes part in, paid only when the swap happens:

1. **Leg.** The fee is on the taker leg when `offer.get` is `"bsv"`, and on the maker leg otherwise. The taker bears it either way: on the taker leg it locks `pay + fee`, and on the maker leg it receives `receive − fee`.
2. **Amount.** `floor(a × 50 / 10000)` (0.5%), where `a` is `pay` on the taker leg or `receive` on the maker leg. There is no fee when that is less than 10 times the cost of one claim on the fee leg's chain.
3. **Output.** A second lock output on the fee leg (§5), claimable by the fee key with the same secret. If the swap happens, the secret is public and the fee key's holder can claim it. If it does not, the output returns to its payer with the leg's refund. On BSV the payee's co-signature covers this refund too.
4. **Agreement.** The terms carry `fee`, and the maker's check (§4 rule 4) refuses any other amount, so neither side can change it. Both wallets MUST agree on the fee key before the take. This version has no field for it (Open questions).

No one holds the fee before the swap happens. A fee holder that never claims leaves the output to return to its payer at the lock time, and a wallet SHOULD then refund it.

**Fee claimer (informative).** A fee holder finds fee outputs to claim by having either wallet report the fee lock: chain, txid, output indexes, amount, hash, payer and payee keys, and lock time. The report is only a pointer. The claimer rebuilds both scripts, checks the lock on the chain, waits for the sibling main output to be claimed, reads the secret from that claim, and claims the fee. A main output that was refunded means there was no swap, and the claimer leaves the fee output alone.

### 9. Application access

The swap methods are the wallet's own. An application cannot take, accept, publish or cancel an offer, or derive any key under the three reserved protocols. A lock counts against the user's payment scope in its asset's profile (draft BRC-214) like any other payment from that wallet.

## Security considerations

- **Atomicity.** One secret opens both legs. The taker reveals it only by claiming the maker leg, before `t2`. The maker then has until `t1`, at least 24 hours later, to claim the taker leg. Neither side can claim without paying unless the other stays away past its own deadline.
- **Order of locks.** The taker locks first, for longer. A maker that never locks costs the taker only its time, and the taker refunds at `t1`. A maker locks only once the taker's lock is buried and matches the terms.
- **Burial.** A wallet never locks or claims against an unburied counterparty lock. On BRC-213 chains depth is counted on the wallet's own header chain, so a lying source can delay a swap but never make a shallow lock pass.
- **BSV refunds.** A BSV lock's refund needs both keys, and the payee signs only a refund carrying the lock time. Miners do not accept a transaction whose `nLockTime` is in the future, so the refund cannot be mined before the lock time.
- **Clock and fee races.** A claim near a deadline can lose a race with a refund. The margins of §5 (8 hours to fund, 3 hours to claim, 24 hours between `t2` and `t1`) cover several blocks on every chain here, including congestion and fee bumps. A wallet SHOULD pay at least the current fee rate on every claim.
- **Message hosts.** A host can delay, drop or replay messages. Every message is sealed and checked against the swap's state, and a missing message never decides an outcome.
- **Offer abuse.** Anyone can take an offer, and a take that the taker never funds costs the maker nothing: it locks only after the taker's lock is buried. A maker answers a given offer once.
- **Privacy.** Both legs carry the same hash, which links them for anyone watching both chains. This version accepts that link.

## Rationale

- **Hash and time locks.** They are the only construction that is atomic on chains that cannot read each other. Both sides use scripts every chain here already accepts.
- **Co-signed refunds on BSV.** BSV restored the original opcodes and has no `OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY`. A refund pre-signed with `nLockTime` gives the same guarantee without a new opcode.
- **36 and 12 hours.** That is long enough for the deepest burial here (20 Dogecoin blocks, or 6 at Litecoin's pace) plus broadcast, and short enough that a failed swap does not tie up coins for days.
- **Fee as an output.** A fee taken by a party in the middle would make that party custodial. An output opened by the swap's own secret makes the fee exactly as conditional as the swap.
- **Offer domain string.** The domain tag keeps an offer signature from being valid as anything else signed under the same key.

## Open questions

1. **Fee key in the terms.** `fee` should name its claim key (`fee.pub`) so that wallets of different makers can charge their own fee. The current implementation fixes it.
2. **Linking.** Adaptor signatures could replace the hash lock and remove the on-chain link between the legs, where both chains support them.
3. **Partial fills.** An offer with `minGet < maxGet` can be taken once in this version. It could be refilled for the rest.
